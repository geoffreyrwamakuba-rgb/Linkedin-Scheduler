Reading a CSV in pandas chunks? Averaging each chunk's mean gives you the wrong overall mean.

With chunksize=, pandas hands you the file in pieces. The tempting move is to take the mean of each piece, then average those.

That only works when every chunk is the same size, and the last chunk usually isn't.

Small example: chunk A has 3 rows with a mean of 10. Chunk B has 1 row with a mean of 50. Average the two means and you get 30. The real mean is (3 × 10 + 50) / 4 = 20.

On real data, 100,000 rows with chunksize=30_000 gives four chunks: 30k, 30k, 30k and 10k rows. Averaging the four means gives each chunk a quarter of the vote, so the 10k chunk counts for 25% when it holds only 10% of the rows.

→ Shuffled file: every chunk is a random mix, so all four means sit near 50. Giving the small one extra weight barely moves the answer. Mean of means 49.93, true mean 49.90. Close enough to pass a test.

→ Same rows sorted by amount: the last chunk now holds the 10,000 largest amounts, with a mean of 94.91. Its extra weight pulls the answer up to 57.40, against a true 49.90. 15% too high.

So the error only shows up when chunks differ from each other, and a file sorted by date, customer or region often has chunks that do.

The fix is to carry a running sum and a running count across chunks, then divide once at the end:

total, rows = 0, 0
for chunk in pd.read_csv("orders.csv", chunksize=30_000):
    total += chunk["amount"].sum()
    rows += len(chunk)
mean = total / rows

Median has no fix like this. The median of the chunk medians isn't the median of the file, so for that you need a tool that can see the whole column at once.
