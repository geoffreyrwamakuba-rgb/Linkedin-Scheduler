Before you add a single column to a fact table, finish this sentence: one row in this table is one what?

That sentence is the grain. Kimball's design process puts declaring it second, after choosing the business process and before any dimensions or measures.

Take one order with three products and £5 shipping.

→ At order grain there is one row. Shipping is £5, which is right. Product doesn't fit, because there are three of them.

→ At order line grain there are three rows. Product fits. But if shipping is copied onto every line, summing it gives £15.

Both grains are valid. The trouble starts when a measure that belongs to one is stored at the other.

Two honest fixes for the shipping case:

→ Keep it at the grain it belongs to, in an order-level fact table.

→ Or split it across the lines on purpose: £1.67, £1.67 and £1.66, so it still adds up to £5. Then write down that it's an allocation.

So, for the fact table you're working on right now: what is one row?
